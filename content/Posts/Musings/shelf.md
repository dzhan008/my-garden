I built a shelf a few weeks back in an effort to spruce up my apartment and organize my knick-knacks in any place other than the floor. I am particularly proud of this shelf, despite a bit of damage incurred to one of the boards while I was constructing it. Here's a picture of the finished product:

PICTURE OF SHELF HERE
(For those interested, this is an Ikea LAIVA bookcase)

From the looks of it, this is a standard budget shelf that you could probably buy anymore. It didn't take long to actually build the shelf (though making heavy furniture alone is *quite* painful sometimes), but the decision to build the shelf was long awaited. Specifically, I had actually ordered the shelf in 2025 Oct (TODO: VERIFY DATE), roughly about a monthish after I moved apartments. My previous unit didn't have a shelf, but I had accumulated random items that I intended to display somewhere, someday. While some of them fit on my desk, there was so much I could put on without creating much of a mess. All in all, I decided that once I moved, I'd commit to buying the shelf so I can finally place all of my goodies in one place.

And so my move happened and I bought the shelf. It came within a week or so in a box, and I had left it in my front entrance to eventually assemble it. But that plan came much, much later, precisely 4-5 months after I actually bought it. What delayed it was initially laziness to eventually a bit of a crisis that made me question myself and my future. This shelf, despite it being another piece of furniture I would build, would eventually harbor some symbolism that I didn't realize until recently.

Laziness aside, I was actually looking for work during my stay in Japan as a student. I was told that looking for work as early as possible would net you the best chances at getting a job, so I had given myself a half year buffer to attempt the hunt. I started getting back into the interview grind and despaired over the number of interviews I'd have to prepare for especially after being rusty at programming. I'm not fond of tech interviews since they normally last 4-5 rounds until you get your final result, and getting rejected midway is a letdown especially when you would have spent roughly a month in communications with your company. As I was prepping for interviews and studying at school, I kind of let the unopened bookcase box in my entrance for a few months longer just to deal with it in the new year.

Unfortunately, I didn't get any offers from my search. I had honestly hoped I would of found something within 3 months, and I had gotten close with two companies. It was particularly despairing when one of my target companies rejected me at my final interview- what had been such a good restart to my tech career became another dashed opportunity. Having been through multiple rejections before this isn't anything new, but it always hurts when you go so far just to fail at the finish line.

I wasn't as worried, but I definitely felt disappointed that I couldn't get anything sooner. I had 3 months left to graduate, though I knew there would be an opportunity to extend my visa 6 months further to find work. However, what I had thought actually turned out to be a mistake.
